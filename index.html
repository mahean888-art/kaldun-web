Every consequential choice is a bet on a future that hasn’t happened yet.

People make those bets every day — in memos, models and meetings — and almost never keep score.

Every claim Kaldun commits is sealed before the outcome exists — hashed, timestamped, and anchored where Kaldun cannot quietly edit it. When reality resolves the claim, the seal opens, the score posts, and the model learns.

Project 1654

1654

Systems that learn from time.

In 1654, Pascal and Fermat traded letters about how to divide the stakes of an unfinished game — and began the mathematics of probability. The unfinished game is now the world.

Project 1654 is Kaldun’s fellowship for researchers building machines whose claims time can test.

Apply to Project 1654 ↗
Who it is for

Researchers and builders who want their work scored by reality rather than by reviewers alone.

What fellows receive

A salary or stipend; a compute budget; access to Kaldun’s live state and the Record as experimental infrastructure; publication of the work; a direct path to the founding research team.

Every fellow’s project must commit at least one claim to the Record. Nothing at Kaldun goes ungraded.
