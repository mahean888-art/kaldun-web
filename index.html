02 — The problem II
What models predict

Language models predict
what comes next in text.

What decisions depend on

Consequential decisions depend on
what comes next in the world.

A prediction is an answer.
Foresight is a belief that survives over time.

Next-token prediction produces remarkably fluent answers. But fluency is not a stable belief about the future.

A decision requires more: possible outcomes, calibrated odds, connected consequences, and a view that changes when the evidence changes.

This is not a claim that language models cannot forecast. They can produce predictions. The deficiency is that their default form does not maintain a persistent, calibrated, continuously tested view of the future.

The next token is not the next state.

04 — The thesis IV

Most AI learns from the past.
Kaldun learns from the future
as it arrives.

Every new signal updates the present. Every resolved outcome becomes a new training example.

The result is a system that does not merely generate predictions. It becomes better at anticipating reality through time.

Time is the supervision signal.

    Selected public and customer-authorized forecasts are cryptographically committed before the outcome is known. The commitment proves that the original forecast, odds, information cutoff, model version, and resolution rules were not rewritten after the fact.
