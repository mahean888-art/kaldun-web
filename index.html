Manifesto

Every decision contains a model of the future.

An investment memo, a credit file, a board strategy, an operating plan — each contains a model of a world that does not exist yet.

Today, that model is dispersed. It lives across spreadsheets, slide decks, analyst notes, market prices, policy assumptions, and individual judgment. It is revised unevenly, separated from the decision it informs, and rarely measured after the fact.

The conditions around those decisions are changing faster than the institutions built to make them. Capital moves across systems continuously. Supply, energy, information, and policy travel through tighter networks. Rules fragment across jurisdictions. A change in one system can alter the conditions of another before the next planning cycle begins.

Software learned to retrieve, summarize, and answer. It did not learn to maintain a state of the world around a consequential decision — and show how that state may change when an institution acts.

We build foresight machines.

A foresight machine maintains an explicit state of what is known now. It advances that state through evidence, time, and possible interventions. It produces distributions — not declarations. It shows the assumptions that matter, the tail conditions that break a decision, and the actions that remain sound across more than one future.

It then commits its claims before the outcome is known.

When reality resolves, the machine is scored. The record remains. The next decision begins with a better model of the world.

Not one future.
Better commitments across many.

The Machine

The next token is not the next state.

A language model continues a sequence. A foresight machine advances a state — under the action you are contemplating, against the evidence it can inspect.

It does not offer a single asserted future. It runs the present through possible conditions, makes uncertainty visible, and keeps the chain of evidence and assumptions attached to the result.

A language model P( tokent+1 | tokens )

A foresight machine P( St+1…t+n | St, A, E, M )

S — current state · A — contemplated action · E — evidence · M — mechanism and assumptions

Where cause can be identified, the Machine shows its method. Where it cannot, it shows its assumptions.

The Record

Made before
the answer was known.

Every claim the Machine makes is committed before the outcome is known: the state snapshot, evidence, assumptions, forecast distribution, and model version are hashed, timestamped, and preserved in an append-only record.

When reality resolves, the claim is evaluated against what happened. The record shows what the Machine knew, what it assumed, what changed, and how the next decision should improve.

A decision should not disappear after it is made.

The Record is the training loop.

Research residency

1654

Systems that learn from time.

In 1654, Pascal and Fermat began formalizing how to reason about an unfinished game. The unfinished game is now the world: institutions, markets, infrastructure, and decisions evolving before their outcomes are known.

Project 1654 is Foresight Machines’ residency for researchers and builders whose systems make claims that time can test.

Every project must commit a claim before the outcome is known. Every project is evaluated after resolution.

Nothing at Foresight Machines goes ungraded.
