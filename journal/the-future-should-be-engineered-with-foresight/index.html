The future should be engineered with foresight

IThe problem

Humanity has built extraordinary systems for recording the past and managing the present.

We have databases that preserve what happened, financial systems that transfer value, software that organizes institutions, and markets that coordinate millions of decisions.

But the systems through which we make consequential commitments about the future remain remarkably primitive.

We allocate capital using incomplete models. We price risk using limited histories. We build infrastructure with uncertain assumptions. We make irreversible decisions without systematically examining the alternatives.

The problem is not that the future is uncertain. It is that our ability to model uncertainty, discover alternatives, and coordinate commitments has not kept pace with the world we have built.

IIWhat changes

The world has outrun the tools we plan with.

Money moves in seconds, supply chains span continents, and power is committed for decades, while the plans that depend on them live in spreadsheets. New risks arrive faster than the histories needed to price them. The commitments are connected; the tools we use to make them are not.

So the most important commitments in the economy are still made one scenario at a time, by people who know the system is bigger than the model.

Artificial intelligence changes what is possible. Machines can now read the contracts, build the models, run the futures, and search the alternatives that no team had time to examine. They now forecast as well as the best human forecasters. In science, they have begun to propose ideas, test them, and learn from what fails. The same can happen for the decisions that shape the economy, because in finance and infrastructure, every rule is written and every outcome settles.

IIIWhat our machines do

We are building Foresight Machines. Every institution will run on one.

A Foresight Machine is built around a single institution. It holds an executable model of that institution and the world it depends on: its money, its contracts, its counterparties, and its rules. No one can experiment on a real institution. Its team can experiment on its machine. Before anyone commits, it answers two questions: what could happen, and what would it take?

It simulates what could happen. It finds the change that holds: a sequence that makes a project possible, a structure that makes a risk bearable, or an arrangement that keeps a promise when conditions change.

It improves itself. It records what it expects before events unfold, checks itself against what happens, and rewrites its own model when it misses. A machine in its third year has a record no team can match.

It starts by answering questions. Then it watches the decisions that recur. Then, as its record earns it, it acts. People set the goals. People decide how far it goes.

When two institutions each run one, their machines can work out terms that hold for both. And a machine that can price a decision can price a risk.

IVWhat we believe

We are not building an oracle. We are building machinery for better judgment.

The future cannot be known completely. But it can be understood more deeply, explored more systematically, and shaped more responsibly.

Every institution keeps a ledger for what happened. Every institution will keep a Foresight Machine for what happens next.

Make foresight infrastructure for the financial and physical world.
