Introducing Foresight Machines

A power project can be valuable and still be impossible to finance under the terms proposed. Different construction stages, supply agreements, or financing conditions might make it viable. Finding that arrangement requires understanding how the pieces interact before anyone commits.

The same problem appears across the economy. An insurer must decide which risks it can carry and on what terms. A manufacturer must decide how to secure supply. A financial institution must place funds where they can meet obligations as conditions change. Each decision concerns resources, uncertainty, and the responses of others.

Institutions have sophisticated tools for these questions. But the evidence sits across contracts, operating systems, forecasts, specialist models, and people. Connecting it takes work. Keeping the connections current takes more. The alternatives considered are often limited by what the team has time to investigate.

Foresight Machines is building AI to expand that search. Our systems will model financial and physical systems, explore possible futures, and investigate better ways to allocate capital and coordinate resources. The aim is to help institutions discover arrangements they can responsibly put into practice.

The model is central. It represents the resources available, the obligations to be met, and the rules and dependencies that govern what happens next. A liquidity model includes payment deadlines, ownership, transfer limits, and settlement times. A model of an infrastructure project may include equipment capacity, construction dependencies, energy availability, and financing conditions. The institution supplies the objectives and authority for the decision.

Simulation runs the model under different conditions and interventions. A machine can investigate how a decision performs as demand, availability, prices, or behavior change. It can search for different timing, terms, partners, or allocations, test a proposed arrangement, identify what defeats it, and use that failure to propose another. Forecasts inform the conditions explored; the model connects those conditions to the choices available.

AI is changing the economics of this work. In the Summer 2026 Metaculus Cup, AI systems finished first and second overall among 885 forecasters. That is a significant result for machine forecasting, even as performance must be assessed on the particular questions a decision requires.

Language models and coding agents can also help extract rules, build models, investigate inconsistencies, and propose tests. Systems such as AlphaEvolve show how AI can generate candidates and improve them through automated evaluation. Applying that discipline to institutions requires models whose assumptions and limits can be examined. It also requires checking whether a modeled improvement survives outside the model.

SourceGoogle DeepMind on AlphaEvolve

We are starting with the liquidity behind stablecoin redemption. An issuer can have sufficient reserves overall while funds are unavailable on the route that needs to pay. We will test how reserve placement, funding routes, service commitments, and contingency terms work together—and investigate changes that improve reliability, reduce cost, or make a new service feasible.

This first application gives us recurring decisions, identifiable obligations, and operating mechanisms that can be checked. It also makes the distinction between an existing option and a proposed arrangement concrete. A transfer may be available today. A new facility requires agreement, onboarding, and proof that it can be used when needed.

Our evaluation will begin with the mechanisms and the evidence behind them. We will compare policies with the institution's current approach and strong conventional methods under the same constraints. Recommendations and expected observations will be recorded before events unfold. Operational exercises will test whether a plan can actually be carried out.

As outcomes arrive, the record should expose where the model was wrong. A proposed correction must improve performance on separate cases before it is relied upon. Simulations estimate what alternatives could produce; they do not reveal the true outcome of every path. The institution sets the objectives, chooses the trade-offs, and retains authority over commitments.

The longer research question is how much of this machinery can travel. Liquidity, collateral, and credit connect to the financing of inventory, equipment, energy, and construction. They share obligations, resources, timing, and dependencies, while each introduces rules and evidence of its own. We intend to develop methods that carry useful knowledge between systems and measure what must be rebuilt. Controlled training and evaluation environments will help us test that transfer.

Success would mean more than improving an existing allocation. A different sequence of purchases might make a project financeable. Different supply terms might make production reliable. Better-designed cover might let an institution carry a risk it would otherwise decline. These are possibilities to investigate and establish through evidence.

We begin in finance because the first decisions must be specific enough to test. The ambition reaches wherever institutions commit resources to an uncertain future. Our mission is to expand what humanity can responsibly make possible.
