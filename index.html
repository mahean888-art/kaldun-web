Mission

Why foresight, and what it is for

Fig 02 · The field of futures

Our mission

Expand what humanity can responsibly make possible.

There’s forecasting, and there’s foresight.

Forecasting anticipates the conditions ahead, and machines now do it about as well as the best people.

But every commitment sits inside a system larger than the decision itself: other participants, agreements, constraints, and the conditions they meet. The outcome turns on how they move together.

Foresight runs that system forward, then searches for a better arrangement: a different allocation, new terms, a sequence that makes a project possible.

Models like these took specialists months to build. AI is making them fast to build and practical to keep current.

Domains

Where the same method applies next

Wherever capital is committed.

Every domain gets the same three questions: what happens if conditions change, what changes if we act differently, and what arrangement could make this work.

  1. A

    Reserves & liquidity

    Issuers, payment networks and funds that owe cash on demand

    1. 01What if partner balances grow 40% next year?
    2. 02Which bank should carry our after-hours redemptions?
    3. 03What would let us redeem instantly, any hour?

    Typical findingCash sits where redemptions don’t come from.

    WhoTreasurer

  2. B

    Credit & collateral

    Lenders, prime brokers and managers whose collateral moves with rates and prices

    1. 01What if rates stay higher for two more years?
    2. 02Which collateral should we post, and where?
    3. 03What terms let us lend where we decline today?

    Typical findingOne sector’s refinancing wall, not the portfolio average.

    WhoChief credit officer

  3. C

    Risk & insurance: new risks

    Insurers and reinsurers writing risks with little loss history: cyber, AI, digital assets, shifting climate

    1. 01What would one provider’s outage cost across our whole book?
    2. 02Where should a parametric trigger sit, and how should the cover be layered?
    3. 03Could we cover a risk the market won’t write today?

    Typical findingThe loss sits in the shared dependency, not the policyholder.

    WhoChief underwriting officer

    A single software update in July 2024 cost Fortune 500 firms an estimated $5.4B; most of it was uninsured.

  4. D

    Projects & supply chains

    Developers and manufacturers whose plans depend on physical delivery

    1. 01What if equipment arrives six months late?
    2. 02More inventory, or a second supplier?
    3. 03What makes this financeable at today’s rates?

    Typical findingThe penalty clause binds, not the loan.

    WhoHead of project finance

Trust

How models earn it

Models earn trust against reality.

Every recommendation shows its sources, its assumptions, and how confident the model is.

Record and revision

  1. 01

    Built from evidence.

    Rules and relationships trace to operating records, contracts and observed behavior. Missing evidence is marked, not filled in.

  2. 02

    Tested against alternatives.

    Each recommendation is compared with current practice and strong conventional methods under the same objectives and constraints, including the conditions under which it fails.

  3. 03

    Kept current.

    The model is updated as contracts, counterparties and conditions change. Every version is archived, so any past recommendation can be reproduced.

  4. 04

    Corrected by outcomes.

    Expectations are recorded before events unfold. Misses become proposed revisions, and a revision must improve results on separate cases before it is used.

LoopEvidence→Alternatives→Current→Outcomes→Evidence
