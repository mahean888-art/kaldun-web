The future should be engineered with foresight

IThe problem

Humanity has built extraordinary systems for recording the past and managing the present.

We have databases that preserve what happened, financial systems that transfer value, software that organizes institutions, and markets that coordinate millions of decisions.

But the systems through which we make consequential commitments about the future remain remarkably primitive.

We allocate capital using incomplete models. We price risk using limited histories. We build infrastructure with uncertain assumptions. We make irreversible decisions without systematically examining the alternatives.

The problem is not that the future is uncertain. It is that our ability to model uncertainty, discover alternatives and coordinate commitments has not kept pace with the world we have built.

IIWhat changes

Money now moves in seconds, while the systems that back it still close at night.

Supply chains span continents, while the plans that depend on them live in spreadsheets. New risks arrive faster than the histories needed to price them. The commitments are connected; the tools we use to make them are not.

So the most important commitments in the economy are still made one scenario at a time, by people who know the system is bigger than the model.

Artificial intelligence changes what is possible. Machines can now read the contracts, build the models, run the futures and search the alternatives that no team had time to examine. In science, they have begun to propose ideas, test them, and learn from what fails. The same can happen for the decisions that shape the economy.

IIIWhat our machines do

We are building Foresight Machines. Every institution will have one.

A Foresight Machine is built around a single institution. It holds a working model of that institution: its money, its contracts, its counterparties, its rules. Before anyone commits, it answers two questions: what could happen, and what would it take?

It simulates what could happen. It discovers what could work: a sequence that makes a project possible, a structure that makes a risk bearable, an arrangement that keeps a promise when conditions change.

It improves itself. It records what it expects before events unfold, checks itself against what happens, and rewrites its own model when it misses. A machine in its third year knows its institution better than any team could.

It starts by answering questions. Then it watches the decisions that recur. Then, as its record earns it, it acts. People set the goals. People decide how far it goes.

When two institutions each have one, their machines can work out terms that hold for both.

IVWhat we believe

We are not building an oracle. We are building machinery for better judgment.

The future cannot be known completely. But it can be understood more deeply, explored more systematically, and shaped more responsibly.

Every institution keeps a ledger for what happened. Every institution will keep a Foresight Machine for what happens next.

Our mission is to make foresight infrastructure for the financial and physical world.
