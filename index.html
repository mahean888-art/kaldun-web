Foresight Machines

There’s forecasting, and there’s foresight.

Forecasting anticipates the conditions ahead, and machines now do it about as well as the best people.

But every commitment depends on a system larger than the decision itself: other participants, agreements, constraints, and the conditions they meet. What happens depends on how they move together.

Foresight runs that system forward, then searches for a better arrangement: a different allocation, new terms, a sequence that makes a project possible.

Building models like these used to take months of specialist work. AI is changing the economics of that work.

Foresight Machines is building AI to expand the choices institutions can make, and the futures those choices make possible.

From what could happen to what could work.

Three questions a model of your system can answer.

  1. Follow a change through the system to see which dependencies and commitments it affects.

    • LiquidityIf our second settlement bank goes offline on Friday, can we still meet Monday’s redemptions?
    • InfrastructureIf equipment arrives six months late, which obligations fall due before revenue begins?
    • SupplyIf a supplier’s lead time doubles, where does our capacity run short first?

  2. Compare allocations, terms and timing to understand their costs and consequences.

    • PaymentsWhich prefunding policy keeps payouts on time at the lowest carrying cost?
    • TreasuryShould we draw on the facility now, or hold bills and arrange repo if needed?
    • InsuranceOn which terms is this specialty risk worth carrying?

  3. Search for combinations the original plan did not consider, and examine what they require.

    • EnergyIs there a sequence of commitments that still finances the project?
    • ReservesWhich mix of routes and contingency terms meets redemptions at acceptable cost?
    • SupplyCould staged orders and milestone funding make this contract viable?

A model to explore with. A record to improve from.

The institution supplies the objectives and the authority. The machine models the system, searches it, and learns from what happens.

Simulation tests alternatives. Observations test the model.

Models earn trust against reality.

Our standard is evidence: traceable assumptions, comparisons with real alternatives, and a record of what happened after the decision.

  1. Ground the model.Connect its rules and relationships to operating records, contracts and observed behavior. Make uncertainty and missing evidence visible.
  2. Challenge the result.Compare alternatives under the same objectives and constraints. Investigate the conditions under which a recommendation changes or fails.
  3. Improve from outcomes.Record expectations before events unfold. Use the resulting errors to propose revisions, then test those revisions on separate cases.
